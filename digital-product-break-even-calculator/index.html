How to Read the Result

Break-even is a planning threshold, not a sales forecast.

Check the inputs first

Small changes in price, fees, refund rate, or launch costs can move the target. Use a cautious version as well as your expected case.

Recalculate when scope changes

Update the estimate if production costs rise, your platform changes, or you add paid promotion.

This calculator provides estimates for planning and education. It is not financial, tax, or legal advice.

Three Break-Even Rules Worth Remembering

Use net revenue

A $29 sale does not put $29 toward your launch costs. Account for transaction fees and expected refunds.

Price changes compound

A modest price adjustment can reduce the number of sales required, but it may also affect conversion. Test both assumptions.

Track the real launch

Replace estimates with actual costs and sales data once the product is live so the target stays useful.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is the digital product break-even point calculated?

Your break-even point is calculated by dividing your total launch costs by the money you actually keep from each sale after fees and refunds.

Should I include platform and payment-processing fees?

Yes. Use the actual percentage and fixed fee from your provider whenever possible. Presets are examples because provider pricing can change.

Why include an expected refund rate?

Refunds reduce the revenue available to recover your launch costs. A cautious estimate can make the sales target more realistic.

How does the conversion-rate estimate help?

It translates the break-even sales target into an approximate traffic requirement. It is a planning estimate rather than a guarantee of buyer behavior.

Is this calculator free and private?

Yes. It is free, requires no account, and performs its calculations locally in your browser.

Can I copy or save the result?

Yes. Copy the launch report to your notes or download it as a text file for later comparison.
