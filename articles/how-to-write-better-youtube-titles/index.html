How To Write Better YouTube Titles That Earn More Clicks

Build clearer, mobile-friendly video titles with strong keyword placement, visible hooks, and accurate curiosity.

6 min read•Uses: YouTube Title Preview Tool

A strong YouTube title has two jobs: help the right viewer understand the topic and give that viewer a compelling reason to click. The best titles do both without hiding the main promise behind vague language or letting the important words get truncated on smaller screens.

Start with a clear video promise

Before optimizing individual words, define what the viewer will receive. Is the video a tutorial, experiment, comparison, case study, warning, or list? A specific promise is easier to understand and usually easier to title than a broad topic.

Compare “My New Editing Workflow” with “How I Edit a YouTube Video in 30 Minutes.” The second version communicates a process, platform, and concrete outcome. It gives the viewer enough information to decide whether the video is relevant.

Keep the visible hook concise

YouTube allows titles up to 100 characters, but that does not mean every layout displays all 100. Search results, home feeds, sidebars, and mobile devices allocate different widths. A practical target is 45 to 60 characters, with the most important information near the beginning.

Front-load the topic or keyword

Put the recognizable topic close to the beginning. This makes the title easier to scan and helps prevent the searchable phrase from disappearing when the end is clipped. Avoid opening with filler words when a stronger subject can lead instead.

  • Weak: “The Best Way I Found to Get Better at Editing”
  • Clearer: “Video Editing Practice: The Method That Worked”
  • Weak: “And This Is Why My Channel Finally Grew”
  • Clearer: “YouTube Channel Growth: What Finally Worked”

Create curiosity without breaking trust

Curiosity works when the title reveals the subject but leaves an outcome, explanation, or contrast for the video. Clickbait hides too much, exaggerates the result, or promises something the content does not deliver. Sustainable click-through rate depends on both the click and the viewer being satisfied afterward.

Use numbers and power words selectively

Numbers can make a title easier to scan because they suggest structure or specificity. Emotional words can create urgency or tension. Neither is automatically useful. Add them when they clarify the story: “7 Editing Mistakes That Slow Down Every Video” is stronger than adding “INSANE” to a title with no specific value.

Check the title and thumbnail together

The title and thumbnail should cooperate instead of repeating the same sentence. The thumbnail can provide visual tension or a short phrase while the title supplies context. Use the Thumbnail Text Checker to test readability, then preview the title separately for device fit.

A repeatable YouTube title workflow

  1. Write the video’s single clearest promise.
  2. Create three to five title angles: searchable, curiosity-led, authority-led, and educational.
  3. Put the topic or main keyword early.
  4. Remove filler words and repeated ideas.
  5. Preview desktop and mobile truncation.
  6. Compare the title with the thumbnail so they add information rather than duplicate it.
  7. Choose the clearest accurate promise—not merely the loudest version.

FAQ

How long should a YouTube title be?

Aim for roughly 45 to 60 characters as a practical starting point. Actual truncation varies by layout, letter widths, device size, and YouTube experiments.

Should every YouTube title include a keyword?

Use a clear phrase that accurately describes the topic. It should read naturally for humans rather than looking like a list of search terms.

Do numbers always improve CTR?

No. Numbers help when they add specificity, structure, or proof. An arbitrary number does not repair a vague video promise.
