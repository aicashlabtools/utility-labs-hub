How To Price Monthly Retainers

Use a freelance retainer pricing calculator to turn project work into predictable recurring revenue.

The Freelancer Monthly Retainer Pricing Calculator helps you estimate a monthly price, target client count, MRR, annual run rate, and workload sustainability. It is built for freelancers moving from one-off projects to more predictable monthly income.

What a freelance retainer pricing calculator helps you decide

A good retainer price has to cover more than hours. It needs to account for availability, response speed, meetings, reporting, client type, and the profit margin required to make recurring work sustainable.

How to structure a freelance retainer package

  1. Define the client outcome, not just a block of hours.
  2. Set the included monthly hours, calls, response time, and deliverables.
  3. Price the package above your hourly floor because access and priority have value.
  4. Limit the number of clients based on real capacity, not best-case energy.
  5. Put out-of-scope work, billing date, and cancellation terms in writing.

How to pitch a retainer to an existing client

Frame the retainer around continuity. Show the client what becomes easier: faster support, fewer restarts, predictable delivery, cleaner reporting, and a fixed monthly plan instead of scattered project approvals.

How many retainer clients can I manage?

Use your available monthly client hours as the ceiling. If each retainer needs 20 hours and you have 80 client hours available, four clients may be the upper limit before sales, admin, and unexpected requests create pressure.

FAQ

How much should I charge for a monthly retainer?

Start with your hourly floor, expected hours, access level, response time, and client value. Then round into a package that feels easy to understand.

Should retainers be paid upfront?

Yes, in most freelance workflows. Upfront monthly payment protects your calendar and keeps recurring work from turning into unpaid credit.
