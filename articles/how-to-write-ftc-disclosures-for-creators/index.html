How To Write FTC Disclosures For Creators

Disclose affiliate links, sponsored posts, gifted products, paid partnerships, and AI-assisted content clearly before your audience acts.

5 min readUses: Creator FTC Disclosure Generator

FTC disclosure wording matters because audiences need to know when a creator has a financial, product, or partnership relationship behind a recommendation. A disclosure should be clear, early, and easy to notice. It should not be hidden inside a long block of hashtags, buried at the end of a caption, or placed only where a viewer might never look.

Common disclosure situations:
  • Affiliate links
  • Sponsored posts
  • Gifted products
  • Paid partnerships
  • Brand ambassador content
  • AI-assisted content

What a good creator disclosure includes

A useful disclosure names the relationship in plain language. For example, "This post is sponsored by..." is clearer than vague wording like "thanks to..." when the post is paid. "I may earn a commission" is clearer than only dropping a hashtag after several lines of copy.

Good disclosures usually answer three questions: what the relationship is, who the brand or partner is when relevant, and whether the creator may receive money, free products, commissions, access, or another benefit.

Where disclosures should appear

  • YouTube: Say the disclosure verbally near the start and repeat it in the description before sponsor or affiliate links.
  • Instagram: Put the disclosure before the caption truncates and use built-in partnership labels when they apply.
  • TikTok: Use clear caption or on-screen wording early because viewers may not read a full caption.
  • Blog: Place the disclosure before the first affiliate link, sponsored recommendation, or buying prompt.
  • Newsletter: Disclose before sponsored sections, affiliate links, or paid recommendations inside the email.

Disclosure examples

For affiliate links, a clear version might say that the post contains affiliate links and that the creator may earn a commission at no additional cost to the viewer. For sponsored content, the disclosure should state that the content is sponsored by the brand. For gifted products, the disclosure should say the item was provided for free.

Short tags like #ad, #sponsored, #affiliate, and #gifted can help, but they work best when paired with clear placement and plain-language wording.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Hiding the disclosure below "more." Put it where people see it before clicking, buying, or watching the recommendation.
  • Using unclear language. Say sponsored, affiliate, gifted, paid partnership, or commission when that is the real relationship.
  • Relying on one buried hashtag. A single tag at the end of a long caption may not be visible enough.
  • Forgetting verbal disclosure. Video and podcast audiences may never read the description or show notes.

FAQ

Do affiliate links require disclosure?

Yes. If you may earn a commission or receive another benefit, disclose the relationship before someone clicks or makes a purchase decision.

Does YouTube require verbal disclosure?

For sponsored, affiliate, or compensated content, a spoken disclosure near the start is a strong practice because viewers may never open the description.

Can I edit the generated disclosure?

Yes. Edit the generated disclosure so it matches the exact brand, product, platform, and campaign terms.
