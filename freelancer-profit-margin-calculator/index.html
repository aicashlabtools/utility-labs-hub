Frequently Asked Questions
What is a good profit margin for freelancers?
There is no universal percentage. A useful target depends on your service, costs, demand, risk, and business model. The status labels here are planning guidance, not industry benchmarks.
How do I calculate profit margin on a freelance project?
Subtract labor, fees, contractors, software, direct expenses, and allocated overhead from project revenue. Divide the remaining net profit by project revenue and multiply by 100.
What’s the difference between revenue and profit?
Revenue is the project price paid by the client. Profit is what remains after all modeled project costs, including the cost of your own working time.
Should I count my own time as a project cost?
Yes for planning purposes. Treating your working time as a cost helps reveal whether the project compensates you adequately after other expenses.
How do I calculate my internal hourly cost?
Use the hourly value required to cover your compensation and business obligations. It is an internal planning cost, not necessarily the hourly rate shown to clients.
How do payment processing fees affect project profit?
Percentage fees rise with the project price, reduce net profit, and must be included in fee-aware break-even and target-margin calculations.
How do I calculate a break-even freelance project price?
Divide fixed project costs by one minus the payment-fee rate. This is the project price where modeled revenue and total costs are equal.
How much should I charge for a 30% or 40% profit margin?
Divide fixed costs by one minus the fee rate minus the desired margin. The calculation is invalid when the fee rate and target margin total 100% or more.
Should software costs be included in freelance project pricing?
Include project-specific software, assets, licenses, and an appropriate allocation of recurring tools when they materially support delivery.
Are my project details uploaded or stored?
No. The calculator runs locally in your browser and does not upload or store your inputs.
